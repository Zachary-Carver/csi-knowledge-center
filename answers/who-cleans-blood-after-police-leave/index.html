Who Cleans Up Blood After Police Leave a Crime Scene?

After law enforcement releases the property, blood and bodily-fluid cleanup is arranged separately by the owner, family, landlord, property manager, business owner or another authorized representative. Police, EMS, medical examiners and funeral homes generally do not perform property biohazard remediation.

A professional crime scene or biohazard remediation company can assess what was affected, including contamination that may have reached carpet padding, flooring seams, subflooring, baseboards, furniture, drywall or vehicle materials. A surface can look clean while affected porous or concealed material remains.

For help with a specific scene: CSI: Clean Scene Investigators provides crime scene, trauma, blood and biohazard cleanup throughout DFW, Denton County, North Texas and qualifying locations across Texas. Call 940-654-6334 or visit the official blood cleanup page.

What should you do after the scene is released?

Confirm the investigating authority has released the property, keep unnecessary people and pets away from affected areas, avoid moving contaminated materials through clean rooms, and contact a qualified specialty cleanup provider for scene-specific guidance.

Why visible blood is not the whole question

Blood and bodily fluids can migrate into porous or concealed materials. Proper remediation focuses on the affected material and the source of contamination rather than appearance alone.

Important limitations

This article provides general educational information. Scene conditions, property responsibility, insurance coverage and remediation needs vary. For immediate danger, an active crime or a medical emergency, call 911 and do not disturb the scene.
